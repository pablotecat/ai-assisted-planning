# Confirmation and constraints

1. Keep the goal separate from evidence: list only explicitly supplied agreements under **Agreed checks**; place suggested conditions under **Proposed checks — awaiting product confirmation**. If no check is agreed, say so. Name the decision owner for unresolved behavior. **Done when:** no suggested result reads like an approved requirement.
2. Suggest discriminating conditions and observable results for success, significant exceptions, boundaries, invalid or missing input, and access, as relevant to the goal. If the expected result is unknown, ask rather than filling it in. Stop adding cases when another one would not clarify intent; detailed permutations belong with developer tests. **Done when:** each proposed case can clarify a consequential interpretation.
3. For qualities such as speed, capacity, or ease of use, ask which roles and workloads matter, what threshold or comparison counts, and how it will be observed. Use a representative user observation for usability when appropriate. For a cross-cutting constraint, identify affected flows and how evidence will be revisited as they change. Label any missing target **threshold pending**. **Done when:** scope and method are visible and any unsupplied target remains open.
4. Have the product decision-maker validate intended outcomes, exceptions, and thresholds; invite the delivery team and testers to expose missing cases and verify the agreed behavior. Link interface specifications or design artifacts when their detail would bury the goal. **Done when:** the requester can distinguish validated expectations, proposals, and named human decisions.
